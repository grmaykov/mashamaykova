Masha Maykova · Artist

About

Masha Maykova is an artist based in Moscow. Her paintings and graphic works bring together everyday life, interiors, landscapes and the intimate world of human relationships.

Born in Yaroslavl in 1952, she studied at the Abramtsevo School of Industrial Art from 1968 to 1972 and at the Art Faculty of the Moscow Technological Institute from 1973 to 1977. She joined the Union of Artists of the USSR in 1983. Her work has been shown in Russia and internationally, and is held in private collections in Russia, Europe, the United States and Canada.

On Her Art

The Road to What Matters

Consciously or unconsciously, an artist always seeks a way towards perfection in their work. What is perfection? It is more than a perfect composition, an original texture, a carefully considered use of colour or the ideal movement of a figure.

Women, girls and men rest, talk, love one another and form friendships in their own reality — which is also mine. They live in a beautiful harmony, different from the harmony of colour and composition. Here I want to break all the rules and create a harmony of my own for these beautiful, strange people: a world where all is well, without betrayal, hatred or envy, where everyone is sincere and loves one another.

In that world, husbands do not leave their wives and women do not fall out of love with men. Nobody is in a hurry. Children treat those around them with tenderness and respect. Transformed by their love for one another and for life, these people stand before us in their nakedness and innocence, in sadness and solitude, trustingly revealing their sincerity and happiness. Perhaps life is not really like this. But for them, and for me, anything is possible, and we are travelling the same road.

Someone once said that creativity reconciles dreams with reality.

Vitaly Patsyukov — on Masha Maykova’s Work

“Golden smoke rises above us.” — Daniil Kharms

In Masha Maykova’s art, the world in its spiritualised everyday life appears as paradise made real. Its inhabitants never cross the threshold of temptation; they are wholly absorbed in what is natural to them — in sky and earth, in wine and bread, in love and trust in one another. Every moment of their lives is filled with an inexhaustible abundance of feeling, with sympathy and responsiveness all around. Their relationships unfold as a mise-en-scène within the great theatre called life. The artist “acts out” her stories, clearly enjoying the game, becoming at once the playwright, director and performer of every part in her plays. Their variations are endless: from sadness and solitude to freedom and happiness, when love pervades everything and every gesture, every movement brings joy.

Masha Maykova’s theatre has its roots in the commedia dell’arte, where Pierrots and Columbines reign. They move gracefully through their own space and time, drawing close to the masks of the Venetian carnival and shifting into Russia’s Silver Age — into the poetry of Akhmatova and Blok and the productions of Vsevolod Meyerhold, finally taking on the tones of Chekhov’s The Cherry Orchard.

Wherever they may be — in a drawing room, in a festive street crowd or at a café table — they are always in the familiar home of their souls, in a land of enchantment and unbreakable vows. Everything familiar in this intimacy shares in everything else; familiar things surround one another and call one another to mind. The whole world, spinning like the cars of a fairground ride, like a New Year tree, is reflected in the artist’s magical mirrors through what lies close at hand, through a warmth and openness in which everyone can find shelter. It sounds a festive major note in the golden blue of Masha’s palette; its flavour can be tasted in the sweet freshness of the fruit on her table. Its concentration lives in the enchanted harmony of her objects, exquisite in their spontaneity, in the baroque richness of women’s clothing and in the simple, innocent happiness of her lovers.

Being in love is perhaps the central theme of Masha Maykova’s art, in all its vivid, visible fullness, its sensuous immediacy and vulnerability. It permeates the visual organism of her compositions, saturating it completely, entering their ornamental patterns, where line is inseparable from feeling. Its life weaves itself like lace, pausing as though to listen, then once more taking hold of the work’s inner state and its distinctive wholeness, which recalls the patchwork costume of a harlequin.

Every painterly gesture the artist makes contains gratitude, a soothing enchantment with reality, in which reason yields completely to the soul. The imagery of Masha Maykova’s inner world is endlessly improvisational; its forms flow like frames of a film, changing scale and point of view. The genre scenes that constitute her “stories”, outwardly resembling situations from everyday life, pass organically into the realm of myth. A festive gathering or a moment of contemplative solitude is naturally transformed by Masha Maykova into a ritual event, in which a world of simple things is elevated to the status of a canon. In this art without artifice, everyday life becomes a universal and piercing recurrence. It follows the laws of calendar celebrations, stopping time and then allowing its cycles to resume. The images seem to continue beyond the work’s completion, as though “finishing” the painting in a continuation of the artefact. Within this system, the still life recovers its primordial quality, clouds on ready-made wallpaper reveal themselves in natural movement, and everything is filled with life-giving light, restoring life’s true tremulousness.

As we experience the variations in the artist’s compositions, we suddenly find ourselves immersed in a creative abundance of visual discoveries, in their overflowing diversity, where artistic consciousness borders on revelation. Masha Maykova breaks down all the “departmental” barriers between techniques of artistic expression, revealing the possibilities of a purified, natural gaze upon our illusion-filled world. Her personal experience lives in the magical shimmer of the existence that surrounds us, affirming its indisputable reality and the phenomenon of the sensuous in the closest, most immediate layers of experience.

Masha Maykova’s world is one of profound simplicity of spirit, where everything made into an object within our earthly dimensions comes alive again. Looking at her compositions, we are drawn into their inner dimension, becoming witnesses to and participants in a living situation “told” by an author who mediates higher energies. Our feelings enter the space of Masha Maykova’s creative play, where the viewpoint moves freely, like the turning of a children’s carousel.

Vitaly Patsyukov.

Vitaly Patsyukov — on Maria Maykova’s Art

As in the ending of Fellini’s 8½, the whimsical procession of figures in Maria Maykova’s compositions goes on and on: couples in love, romantic young men, flirtatious young women, children. They meet, talk quietly and look out at the world around them, alongside imaginary figures and people from other eras. The artist’s silhouette-like painting recalls a shadow theatre, where constancy reigns and the destructive work of time is held in contempt.

It unfolds through images of an endless carnival, in which lovers play the leading roles. Maria Maykova’s visual dialogues recall poetic images of a bloodless bullfight — the eternal struggle between man and woman, in which the gentle strength of femininity always triumphs. The defeated man traditionally drops to one knee and bows before the victor, tamed and meek. Masks give way to other masks and are exchanged, as though the artist were staging an endless, happy version of Shakespeare’s A Midsummer Night’s Dream, in which youth is forever renewed.

Maria Maykova’s art is never autobiographical in a literal sense. Yet her entire body of work is a diary, continuing from year to year, from day to day. This art was born of an era in which everything shifted from its accustomed place, values were reassessed and matter itself was revealed to consciousness as a flow of energy, an unbroken chain of metamorphoses and mutual transformations. In place of the painting as a self-contained “opus”, she asserts seriality: SUCCESSIONS, chains and cycles of images, changing like frames of old newsreels. These transformations contain much humour and play, but the humour and play are inherent in the very nature of art. In the artist’s system of values, the comic and the great, the naïve and the wise, the animal and the human are not separated by an abyss: they can always exchange places or pass into one another.

Maria Maykova’s preference for asymmetrical constructions is significant, signalling the soul’s freedom and independence. Through the force of her “poetic irony”, she exposes the unfinished nature of today’s world — a world of disturbed proportions, unstable and essentially still infant, still “prehistoric”. Her art is always on a journey towards the unknown; its curiosity is inexhaustible. The artist’s passion is to enquire, to rediscover every object, to name things within every object and phenomenon, and to reveal new aspects of them. For her, pure form does not exist apart from an inner experience of the world’s phenomena. In turning to the language of the “naïve”, she is unwilling to grant the sense of elegance a privileged position and sacrifice the fullness of experience to it. For her, art is something other than the traditional world of beauty: it is always liberation and a “temperature reading”, in which the creative act is entirely organic, flowing as easily and naturally as breathing.

Whatever Maria Maykova does — painting, drawing or book design — she does in complete accord with her nature, following her intuition and changing as the world changes.

Vitaly Patsyukov.
